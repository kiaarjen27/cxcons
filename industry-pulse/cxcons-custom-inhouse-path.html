When to Request a Custom In-House Pathway

Standard modules work when the gap is common and the outline already fits. A custom in-house pathway fits when your SOPs, mixed baselines, or sensitive context would make a generic run miss the point.

Industry Pulse · Working with CXCons

Custom does not mean “longer brochure.” It means the design is shaped around your examples, audience mix, and outcomes — so participants practise what they will face at work, not only a generic case study.

When custom is the better fit

  • Internal examples required — policies, forms, or incidents that cannot be replaced by public cases
  • Multi-department cohorts — different baselines in one room; one fixed outline will under- or over-serve half the group
  • Competency framework links — training must map to your levels, not only a catalogue title
  • Sensitive contexts — post-change, conflict, or confidential operating issues
  • Mixed delivery — workshop + coaching, phased cohorts, or multi-site rollouts under one design

When a standard module is enough

  • The skill is widely shared and the published outline already matches
  • You need speed and a clear brochure to approve budget
  • Audience level is relatively even
  • You are still exploring themes before committing to a pathway

What to prepare before you request

  • Target audience and approximate headcount
  • Outcomes that matter (behaviour, compliance, productivity)
  • Constraints: duration, language, sites, confidentiality
  • Whether HRD Corp claim structure is part of the plan

What to do next

Decide whether you need a catalogue module or a tailored pathway. Assign one internal owner, one success measure, and one target window — then submit a custom request or start from Program Finder if a standard title is close enough.

Bottom line

Request a custom in-house pathway when internal SOPs, mixed baselines, or sensitive context would make a standard run incomplete. Use the catalogue when fit is already good and speed matters more than redesign.
