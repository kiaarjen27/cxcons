Alert: Avoid Costly HRDC Claims & Funding Errors

Compliance Alert: Most lost HRD Corp funding does not happen because a training course lacked value — it happens because of preventable grant, attendance, query, or claim-window errors. Under stricter grant rules and tight response deadlines, a single overlooked detail can turn approved funding into a rejected claim or forfeited levy.

Industry Pulse · HRD Corp compliance alert

Employers often treat grant approval as the finish line, assuming reimbursement will follow automatically once the workshop ends. In practice, HRD Corp audits claims strictly against approved grant details, minimum attendance thresholds, allowable cost ceilings, and statutory submission windows.

High-risk funding and claim errors to watch

  • Starting training before approval or before the lead-time gap — retrospective approvals are not granted, and training must respect mandatory post-approval commencement windows (including the 14-calendar-day lead-time rule for in-house training under Employer’s Circular No. 2/2026)
  • Unapproved changes after grant approval — approved grants cannot be modified for date, venue, trainer, or delivery changes; proceeding after an unapproved change invites claim rejection
  • Attendance below 75% or mismatched trainee records — trainees must attend at least 75% of total training hours, and attendance logs must match approved names, dates, and actual headcount (with in-house course fees prorated if attendance drops below five participants)
  • Unanswered queries or missed 6-month claim deadlines — claims must be submitted within six months of completion, and queries must be answered promptly inside the allowed window before the clock expires
  • Claiming non-allowable items or missing proof — expenses outside the Allowable Cost Matrix or lacking proper invoices, receipts, e-tickets, or properly signed JD/14 forms fail verification
  • Missing the 1-month claim appeal window — if a claim is rejected, any first-time appeal with supporting evidence must be submitted within one month from the rejection date and followed up within five working days when queried

Why claim mistakes slip through internal teams

Handover gaps between training coordinators, finance, and line managers cause most avoidable rejections. When no single owner tracks e-TRiS queries, verifies daily attendance logs, or watches the six-month claim calendar ahead of staff turnover or year-end leave, viable claims stall quietly.

Practical habits to prevent claim losses

  • Assign one clear e-TRiS owner to monitor applications, queries, and claim deadlines from grant submission through reimbursement
  • Lock dates, trainers, and venues before submitting grants so post-approval changes are not needed
  • Verify daily sign-in/sign-out records, 75% hour completion, and JD/14 sign-offs immediately after training ends
  • Set a mandatory internal claim-filing target well ahead of the six-month completion deadline
  • Keep a complete, audit-ready document pack (invoices, receipts, travel/venue proof, attendance, and approvals) before submitting

What to do next

Run one open-claim and grant health check this week: flag any training approaching the six-month claim limit, confirm all active e-TRiS queries are answered, and verify that no unapproved changes slipped into upcoming sessions.

Bottom line

Protecting HRD Corp funding requires disciplined calendar tracking, strict attendance verification, and zero guesswork after grant approval. Tight claim hygiene ensures your company recovers the levy it invests in workforce capability.
