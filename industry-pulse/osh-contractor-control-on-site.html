Contractor Control on Site Is Non-Optional

If contractors work under your direction, their safety is part of your operating system. Signature sheets are not control — induction quality, active supervision, and stop-work authority must match actual site risk.

Industry Pulse · Workplace safety & contractor governance

A signed attendance form without briefed, task-specific hazards is paperwork theatre, not duty of care in practice. When principal employers treat third-party contractors as outside their safety system, operational blind spots quickly turn routine maintenance or project work into serious incidents.

Minimum operating set for site control

  • Risk-matched induction — tailor briefings to the specific trade, work zone, and active hazards rather than relying on a generic slideshow
  • Named, reachable site contact — assign an accountable host supervisor who is accessible throughout the shift
  • Active permit-to-work controls — enforce clear sign-off before high-risk tasks such as hot work, confined spaces, or working at height
  • Aligned incident and near-miss reporting — require contractors to log close calls and injuries directly into the host employer’s system

The supervision reality check

If your line supervisors cannot explain what a contractor crew is doing on site today, where they are working, and under which safety controls, you do not have operational control — you are relying on hope. Host supervision means verifying compliance on the floor, not waiting for a post-incident audit.

Practical operating habits

  • Conduct brief pre-start toolbox checks whenever contractor scope or site conditions shift
  • Verify that both host supervisors and contractor leads understand who holds immediate stop-work authority
  • Audit active permits against actual field conditions rather than checking boxes in the office
  • Include contractor near-misses in weekly site safety reviews without penalising early reporting

What to do next

Establish one risk-specific induction standard, one named host contact per contractor crew, and one unified reporting rule. Walk the floor this week and test whether supervisors can name every active contractor task and its required control.

Bottom line

Outsourcing a task never outsources your duty of care on site. Practical contractor governance protects workers, prevents operational stoppages, and stands up to regulatory scrutiny.
