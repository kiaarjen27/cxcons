Training Needs Analysis (TNA): Plan Before You Spend

A practical briefing on using TNA to prioritise skill gaps, choose the right audience, and align training — including HRDC-ready pathways — with real business goals.

Start Free TNA Program Finder Industry Pulse

Many organisations approve training because a topic is popular, a vendor is familiar, or a budget line must be used before year-end. A Training Needs Analysis (TNA) changes the sequence: first clarify the business goal and skill gaps, then select programmes that fit.

For Malaysian employers — especially those using HRD Corp (HRDC) support — TNA is not bureaucracy. It is how you reduce wasted seats, defend spend internally, and connect learning to performance, compliance, or operational risk.

What TNA is (in workplace terms)

TNA is a structured way to answer:

  • Who needs development (role, team, or level)?
  • What gap matters most (knowledge, skill, or behaviour)?
  • Why now (performance, compliance, safety, customer, change)?
  • How should learning be delivered (public, in-house, online, coaching)?
  • When should results show up (this quarter, six months, this year)?

It sits beside topics such as HRDC claims, OSH duties, and PDPA handling: those define rules and risk; TNA defines where capability investment should go first.

When employers most need a TNA

Before HRDC planning

So claimable programmes map to a documented need — not only to available dates.

After incidents or audits

Safety, quality, or conduct issues often point to supervision and process gaps, not one generic course.

During change

New systems, new managers, or role redesign need prioritised upskilling — not a random catalogue pick.

When budget is tight

TNA forces ranking: what must improve first versus what can wait.

A simple TNA flow that works

  1. Business goal — what should improve if training works?
  2. Gap areas — compliance, leadership, safety, digital, quality, etc.
  3. Audience & level — who attends, and are they beginner or already solid?
  4. Pressure — performance, audit, retention, transformation, customer risk
  5. Priority list — top focus areas with a who / what / when draft plan
  6. Programme match — public schedule, in-house design, or mixed pathway

TNA vs “sending people to a course”

  • Course-first: topic selected → seats filled → impact unclear
  • TNA-first: goal and gaps ranked → audience fixed → programme chosen to fit

The second path is easier to explain to management, finance, and — where relevant — to support a coherent HRDC training story across the year.
