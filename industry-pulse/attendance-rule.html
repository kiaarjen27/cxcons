The 75% Attendance Rule and How Claims Are Prorated

Under Allowable Cost Matrix practice, trainees are generally expected to complete at least 75% of total training hours for full claim entitlement. Below that level, course fees and allowances may be prorated by hours completed — so attendance is not admin only; it is part of the money calculation.

Employers often budget as if every registered name will convert into a full recoverable amount. Operationally, claim value follows what the attendance file can prove: who was present, for which blocks, and for how many hours.

What the 75% rule means in practice

  • Plan against total training hours, not only “days on the calendar”
  • A participant below the completion threshold may not support a full claim line
  • Where proration applies, recoverable fees and allowances track actual hours completed
  • Half-day and full-day structures still depend on real hour blocks delivered

Treat 75% as a design constraint before the room opens: agenda length, break logic, and same-day recording habits all affect whether the file stays clean.

Proration is not only about 75%

Two common proration stories sit beside the attendance threshold:

  • Hours completed: partial attendance can reduce claimable amounts proportionally
  • Small in-house groups: when fewer than five participants attend, the daily group rate may be reduced proportionally under ACM in-house logic

Finance and L&D should model both risks early — especially for specialised runs where last-minute dropouts are common.

Face-to-face vs e-learning evidence

Face-to-face / classroom
Signed attendance by session or day, consistent names, and hours that match the approved outline.

E-learning / remote online
System-generated attendance with timestamps is often used to calculate hours completed. Employers should still retain records they can explain if verification is requested.

Where provider and employer declarations are required for online attendance, complete them as part of close-out — not weeks later from memory.

Operating habits that protect the claim

  • Capture attendance by block or day during the programme
  • Do not “smooth” partial attendance after the event
  • Keep title consistency across outline, attendance, and invoice family
  • File signed or system reports into the programme folder the same day
  • Flag dropout risk before training day when headcount is near the minimum

Bottom line

The 75% rule converts attendance into claim arithmetic. Strong delivery with weak hour evidence still becomes a weaker recovery. Track hours carefully, retain signed or system records, and assume HRD Corp may ask for verification before the final amount is approved.
