Handling Misconduct: The Art of the "Due Inquiry"

Terminating an employee for misconduct is one of the highest-risk activities for a manager. The Employment Act 1955 requires a "Due Inquiry" before any punishment—like dismissal or downgrading—can be legally enforced.

Section 14 of the Employment Act is clear: you must conduct a proper inquiry before dismissing someone for misconduct. Skipping this process or conducting it poorly often leads to expensive "Unfair Dismissal" claims in the Industrial Court.

Many managers lack the confidence to conduct these inquiries.
