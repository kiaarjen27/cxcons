HRDC Claim Rejected: Common Reasons Employers Miss

Most rejections are process failures, not “bad training.” The same five file problems show up across employers.

A strong classroom day can still fail at claim stage

HRD Corp assesses a file: who attended, for how long, what was delivered, and how costs are evidenced. When those four answers do not line up, approval slows or amount is cut — even if participants rated the session highly.

1. Attendance that cannot be defended

  • Illegible names, missing signatures, or sheets with no programme title/date
  • Headcount on the invoice that does not match the sheet
  • Completion below the logic your scheme expects (often discussed around 75% of hours)
  • Online runs without system-generated evidence where required

2. Invoice story ≠ training story

Dates, entity names, and line items must describe the same event as the outline and attendance. Classic failures: training on 12–13 Nov but invoice for a different window; “training package” lines with no breakdown; wrong billing entity.

3. Missing programme structure evidence

Keep the final outline used in delivery — not only a sales brochure version. Reviewers need something to map against allowable cost logic.

4. Scheme / route assumptions mixed up

Public vs in-house, who invoices whom, and which documents the employer retains must be agreed before the PO — not reconstructed after a query.

5. Documents rebuilt weeks later

Assign one claim owner and close a single folder within 7 days of the last session day.

Minimum pack (every programme run)

  • Final outline · Attendance · Invoice + payment trail
  • Trainer type note · Exception log (withdrawals, short hours, replacements)

Design training that survives claim review
