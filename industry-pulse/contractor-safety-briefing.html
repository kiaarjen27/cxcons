Contractor Safety Briefings Are Not Optional Theatre

Contractors under your direction need induction matched to real site risk — not a signature sheet and a generic video. If the briefing does not change what people do in the first hour on site, it is theatre.

Industry Pulse · Occupational Safety & Health

Principals remain accountable for people working under their direction, including contractors and subcontractors where the legal and operational duty applies. A briefing that only “ticks induction” does not reduce incidents. It creates a file that looks complete until something goes wrong.

What theatre looks like

  • One generic slide deck for every trade and every hazard profile
  • Signatures collected without checking understanding
  • No walk-through of actual high-risk zones on that site
  • Emergency routes, isolation points, and permit rules explained only on paper
  • Subcontractors starting work before induction is finished

What a workable briefing covers

  • Site-specific hazards — not only company policy slogans
  • Permit-to-work / isolation rules where relevant
  • Emergency response — assembly, contacts, first aid, spill or fire triggers
  • Who can stop work and how to escalate without fear of “delaying the job”
  • PPE and access rules for the areas they will actually enter
  • Reporting near misses in a channel that contractors can use

Ownership that holds

  • Site / operations owner: briefing happens before work starts — every time
  • EHS / safety: content matches current hazards and legal expectations
  • Procurement / contract owner: safety induction is a contract condition, not a favour
  • Supervisor on shift: verifies the right people were briefed for that day’s scope

Practical habits (30–90 day check)

  • Is induction recorded by name, company, date, and scope of work?
  • Can a random contractor name the emergency route and stop-work contact?
  • Are high-risk tasks delayed until briefing is complete — in practice, not only in policy?
  • After an incident or near miss, was the briefing content updated?

Bottom line

Contractor safety briefings are part of operational control, not optional theatre. Match induction to real site risk, require it before work, and treat understanding — not signatures alone — as the standard.
