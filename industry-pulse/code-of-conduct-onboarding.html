Code of Conduct Belongs in Onboarding Week

New hires absorb culture early. If ethics only appears in month six, informal norms win first. Week one is when “what counts here” is decided — by what leaders emphasise, joke about, and ignore.

Industry Pulse · Workplace conduct

A code of conduct e-sign in a portal is not the same as a short, serious briefing with scenarios. When conduct training is delayed, new hires learn the real rules from WhatsApp groups, lunch tables, and “how we do things in this department.” Catching up later means unlearning — which is slower and less reliable.

Acknowledgement is not understanding

An electronic tick proves receipt. It does not prove the person can handle a grey zone:

  • A vendor offers a festive hamper during a quotation period
  • A colleague asks to share a client list “just for reference”
  • Someone suggests changing a timesheet “because the system is unfair”

Those moments need practised judgement — not a slogan slide.

Sample 25-minute onboarding briefing

You do not need a half-day workshop in week one. You need a focused conversation:

  • 0–5 min: Why this company takes conduct seriously (one real risk story, not fear theatre)
  • 5–12 min: Four non-negotiables — respect, honesty in records, conflicts of interest, speak-up routes
  • 12–22 min: Two grey-zone scenarios with group discussion
  • 22–25 min: Where to report, what happens next, and who the first contact is

Two scenarios you can use immediately

Scenario A — Hospitality during a vendor decision
A supplier sends a premium gift set to a new executive’s home address “as a welcome.” A tender involving that supplier is open.

Better response direction: do not accept quietly; declare; follow gift policy; remove yourself from the decision if required.

Scenario B — “Just help the team” data request
A friendly colleague asks for a customer contact export “to close the month faster,” outside normal system access.

Better response direction: decline informal exports; use approved channels; escalate if pressured. Helpfulness is not a reason to bypass controls.

Hiring manager: 30-day reinforcement checklist

HR can brief. Managers make it real:

  • Week 1: Confirm the new hire knows the speak-up route (name + channel)
  • Week 2: In 1:1, ask: “What feels unclear about our rules so far?”
  • Week 3: Point out one positive example of good conduct on the team
  • Week 4: Check understanding of gifts, attendance honesty, and data sharing

What “decorative” looks like

  • PDF only, no discussion
  • Training scheduled months after joining
  • Managers joking that “HR is overdoing it”
  • Inconsistent enforcement by seniority

Staff notice all four quickly. Culture is what happens after the slide deck closes.

Bottom line

Put conduct in onboarding week, use scenarios, and have managers reinforce for 30 days. That is how a code becomes practice — not decoration.
