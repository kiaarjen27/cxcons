Two Trainers on a Two-Day HRDC Programme: Rules & Cost Logic

Splitting a two-day workshop between two specialist trainers — Trainer A on Day 1 and Trainer B on Day 2 — is permitted under HRD Corp guidelines, provided both trainers are declared upfront and the cost logic is structured per group, not per trainer. Treating a trainer handover as an informal last-minute swap creates immediate claim risk.

Industry Pulse · HRD Corp compliance & programme delivery

Multi-topic programmes often benefit from pairing two subject-matter specialists across separate days — for example, one trainer leading statutory principles on Day 1 and another facilitating practical case execution on Day 2. Under HRD Corp’s Claimable Courses and Allowable Cost Matrix framework, splitting delivery across two trainers is workable as long as course registration, grant declarations, and daily fee ceilings align cleanly.

Key HRD Corp rules when using two trainers

  • Daily ceilings apply per group, not per trainer — under the Allowable Cost Matrix, in-house course fee caps (and internal trainer allowances) are calculated per group per training day; having two trainers split Day 1 and Day 2 does not double the daily group fee ceiling
  • Both trainers must be registered under the course in e-TRiS — for registered HRD Corp Claimable Courses, every trainer delivering any part of the programme must be linked to that registered course profile before the employer submits the grant application
  • Individual accreditation and subject alignment — each trainer must hold active HRD Corp Trainer Accreditation (or valid TTT exemption within their designated exemption area) and demonstrate background credentials relevant to the modules they deliver
  • Schedule must specify who teaches which day — the training outline and daily timetable submitted with the grant should clearly map Day 1 modules to Trainer A and Day 2 modules to Trainer B so contact hours (e.g., 7 hours per day) reconcile cleanly
  • Zero post-approval trainer swaps — once a grant is approved, employers and providers cannot swap in a different Day 2 trainer without cancelling the approved grant and submitting a fresh application within allowable lead times

Travel allowances and supporting documents

While the daily course fee cap remains fixed per group per day, legitimate travel or meal allowances under the Allowable Cost Matrix are calculated per eligible trainer per active training day (for example, Trainer A claiming travel distance allowance for Day 1 and Trainer B for Day 2, rather than both claiming for days they are not facilitating). At the claim stage, both trainers’ profiles and the completed attendance and JD14 records must match the exact names approved in the grant.

Practical operating habits

  • Verify that the training provider has added both Trainer A and Trainer B under the registered course in e-TRiS before you initiate the grant application
  • Attach both trainers’ profiles and accreditation certificates alongside a day-by-day schedule showing each trainer’s assigned modules
  • Confirm a backup plan early — if Trainer B is unavailable before approval, update the application immediately rather than waiting until after grant approval
  • Ensure Day 1 and Day 2 attendance logs clearly identify the active facilitator for each respective day

What to do next

Build one pre-grant dual-trainer check into your intake workflow: confirm both trainers’ accreditation status, lock the Day 1 / Day 2 timetable split, and verify that daily fees stay within single-group ceilings before submitting in e-TRiS.

Bottom line

Using different trainers across Day 1 and Day 2 can elevate programme depth without creating grant friction — provided both trainers are declared upfront, accredited for their topics, and budgeted within standard daily group limits.
