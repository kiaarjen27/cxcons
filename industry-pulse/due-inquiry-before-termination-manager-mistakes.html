Due Inquiry Before Termination

Most dismissal disputes are not lost on the final letter. They are lost on the process managers skipped before the letter was signed.

Process is the defence

Under Malaysia’s industrial relations framework, a workman may challenge dismissal as being without just cause or excuse. When that happens, the employer’s file is examined: what was alleged, what notice was given, what evidence was tested, and whether the employee had a real chance to respond. A fast termination with thin records is a high-cost risk.

1. Put the allegation in writing

  • State the specific misconduct or performance issue — not vague labels
  • Include dates, incidents, and relevant policy references where available
  • Give the employee enough detail to answer the case
  • Avoid “catch-all” wording that collapses under scrutiny

2. Give a real opportunity to be heard

  • Invite the employee to a meeting with reasonable notice
  • Allow explanation, documents, and witnesses where appropriate
  • Do not treat the session as a formality after the decision is already fixed
  • Record who attended, what was said, and what evidence was considered

3. Separate investigation from decision

  • Gather facts first
  • Test the employee’s response second
  • Decide third — with reasons tied to evidence
  • Where possible, avoid one person acting as accuser, investigator, and final judge

4. Keep the file that wins or loses the case

  • Show-cause / allegation letter
  • Meeting notices and attendance notes
  • Evidence relied on (emails, CCTV logs, policies, prior warnings)
  • Employee’s written reply, if any
  • Findings and decision reasons
  • Termination letter consistent with the findings

5. Watch protected timing and status

  • Maternity and certain protected periods carry elevated legal risk
  • Probation still requires fairness — not silence then sudden exit
  • Medical and sensitive personal data in the file must be handled carefully
  • Foreign employee terminations may trigger additional notification duties

Manager checklist before any termination letter

  • Is the allegation specific enough to answer?
  • Was the employee heard on those facts?
  • Does the evidence support the finding?
  • Is the penalty proportionate to the proven issue?
  • Is the paper trail complete and consistent?
