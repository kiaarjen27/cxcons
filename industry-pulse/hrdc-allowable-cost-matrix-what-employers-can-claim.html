HRDC Allowable Cost Matrix: What Employers Can Claim

A clear briefing for Malaysian employers on using the HRD Corp Allowable Cost Matrix (ACM) to plan training spend — before the claim stage, not after.

HRD Corp Hub Program Finder Training Needs Analysis

HRDC support is most useful when training is planned against the rules that govern claims. The Allowable Cost Matrix (ACM) is the reference many employers use to understand which cost items may be considered in a claim — and which usually fall outside it.

This briefing is a practical overview for HR and learning teams. Always check the latest HRD Corp circulars and guidelines for your scheme and training format before you commit budget.

Why the ACM matters in planning

  • Helps you design a programme that is claim-ready from day one
  • Reduces surprise gaps between “quoted training cost” and “claimable items”
  • Supports clearer internal approval when finance asks what the levy can cover
  • Encourages better documentation habits during delivery, not only at claim submission

Cost areas employers typically review

Depending on the scheme and current ACM edition, employers often review categories such as:

Course / training fees

The core programme fee for approved or eligible training, subject to scheme rules and limits.

Allowances

Items such as meal or daily allowances may apply in defined situations — always confirm current rates and conditions.

Materials & consumables

Training materials that are necessary for delivery may be considered where the ACM and supporting documents allow.

Venue, travel & logistics

Venue rental or transport-related costs can be relevant for certain formats — eligibility is not automatic.

Planning habits that protect claims

  1. Confirm scheme fit (public, in-house, online, or mixed) before signing the training plan
  2. Align programme title, duration, and audience with what you will later submit
  3. Track attendance properly — incomplete attendance is a frequent claim friction point
  4. Keep invoices, schedules, and supporting documents in one pack from the start
  5. Treat ACM limits as a design constraint, not a post-event negotiation

Common pitfalls

  • Assuming every line item on a vendor invoice is claimable
  • Running training first and reading ACM rules only at claim time
  • Weak attendance or incomplete participant records
  • Mismatch between approved plan details and actual delivery
  • Relying on outdated ACM tables after a circular update
