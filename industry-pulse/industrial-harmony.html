Industrial Harmony & Dispute Resolution

The Industrial Relations Act 1967 (Act 177) aims to promote and maintain industrial harmony in Malaysia. It regulates the relationship between employers, workmen, and trade unions, providing the framework for collective bargaining and the resolution of workplace grievances.

Section 20 is a vital component of this Act, allowing employees to challenge dismissals they deem to be without "just cause or excuse." Following the 2021 amendments, the process has been streamlined; representations for reinstatement must be filed within 60 days. If conciliation fails at the Industrial Relations Department, cases are now referred more directly to the Industrial Court for an official award.

The Act also safeguards the right to participate in trade union activities without fear of victimization. It defines "unfair labor practices" and ensures that once a collective agreement is taken cognizance of by the Industrial Court, it becomes legally binding on both parties, effectively serving as a contract that maintains stability in the workplace.
