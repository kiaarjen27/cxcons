PDPA 2010 for HR: Handling Employee Data Responsibly

A practical briefing for Malaysian employers on personal data duties in hiring, employment files, and day-to-day HR operations.

Industry Pulse Program Finder Training Needs Analysis

HR teams handle personal data every day — applications, IC details, bank information, medical notes, performance records, and exit files. Under Malaysia’s Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (PDPA), that information is not only an admin asset; it is regulated personal data that must be collected, used, stored, and shared with care.

This briefing is a workplace-focused overview for HR and managers. It is not legal advice. For high-risk cases, seek qualified counsel and check the latest official guidance.

Where employee data usually appears

  • Recruitment forms, CVs, interview notes, and offer packs
  • Personnel files, payroll, benefits, and claims records
  • Attendance, performance, disciplinary, and grievance documents
  • CCTV, access cards, and IT system logs linked to identifiable staff
  • Vendor or insurer sharing for benefits, medical, or audit purposes

Principles HR should operationalise

Notice & purpose

Staff should understand what data you collect and why — in language that matches real HR processes, not only a generic privacy paragraph.

Use limitation

Data collected for payroll or recruitment should not drift into unrelated uses without a proper basis and clear communication.

Security

Access should be limited to roles that need it. Shared drives, open email threads, and unlocked cabinets remain common weak points.

Retention

Keep records for as long as there is a clear business or legal need — then dispose of them securely instead of storing “everything forever”.

Practical gaps we still see

  • CVs and IC copies sitting in shared folders with wide access
  • Managers forwarding employee details on personal chat apps
  • No clear rule for how long failed-candidate data is kept
  • Exit files left incomplete while system access remains open
  • Privacy notices that do not match actual HR forms and vendors

A simple HR operating checklist

  1. Map where employee personal data is collected and stored
  2. Align notices and forms with real purposes (hire, pay, benefits, discipline)
  3. Restrict access by role; review shared drives and HRIS permissions
  4. Set retention periods for recruitment, personnel, and disciplinary files
  5. Train supervisors on what they may share — and what they must not
  6. Document how you respond to access or correction requests

Training angle

PDPA risk is rarely only an IT issue. It becomes operational when supervisors handle complaints, medical notes, or performance records without a shared standard. Short awareness sessions help HR and line leaders reduce everyday exposure.
