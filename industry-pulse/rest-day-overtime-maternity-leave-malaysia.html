Employment Act 1955

The foundation of workplace standards in Malaysia — contracts, wages, hours, leave, and termination. Updated text commonly referenced as at 1 January 2023.

Why this Act still drives HR decisions

The Employment Act 1955 (Act 265) sets minimum conditions of service for covered employees. Most day-to-day issues — contracts, overtime claims, rest-day pay, annual leave, maternity protection, and notice of termination — start here.

Contracts and termination

  • Certain contracts must be in writing and include how the contract may be terminated.
  • Either party may end a contract with proper notice under the Act’s notice rules.
  • Termination without notice is allowed in limited situations, including wilful breach.
  • Misconduct must be handled carefully — employers may terminate for special reasons after due process.
  • Wages due on normal or special termination must be paid within the statutory timelines.

Hours of work, rest days, and overtime

  • Normal daily hours are subject to statutory limits under section 60A.
  • Employees are entitled to a rest day; work on a rest day attracts higher pay rates.
  • Overtime must be paid at not less than one and a half times the hourly rate of pay.
  • “Hours of work” means time when the employee is at the employer’s disposal.
  • Shift work and night work have additional controls employers must observe.

Leave entitlements employers often miscalculate

  • Annual leave — paid entitlement based on completed years of service (starting from 8 days for shorter service bands, rising with tenure).
  • Sick leave — statutory paid sick leave, separate from annual leave.
  • Public holidays — paid holiday rules and substitution practices must be applied correctly.
  • Paternity leave — recognised under the Act’s leave framework for eligible employees.
  • If sick leave or maternity leave falls during annual leave, those days are treated under the relevant leave type, not as annual leave consumed.

Maternity protection

  • Eligible female employees are entitled to maternity leave and maternity allowance for each confinement.
  • Allowance is generally tied to ordinary rate of pay for the eligible period.
  • Terminating a female employee during maternity leave is an offence (except limited situations such as business closure).
  • There are restrictions on terminating pregnant employees except on specified grounds, with the burden of proof on the employer in key situations.
  • Notification failures can affect allowance eligibility — process discipline matters.

Flexible working and foreign employees

  • Employees may apply for flexible working arrangements to vary hours, days, or place of work.
  • Employers must handle such applications under the Act’s process expectations.
  • Foreign employee terminations carry notification duties to the Director General.
  • There are restrictions on terminating local employees to replace them with foreign employees.

Employer action list

  • Audit contracts for notice, hours, and leave language
  • Align payroll overtime formulas with statutory multipliers
  • Keep clean leave and attendance records
  • Train managers on maternity and termination restrictions
  • Document every high-risk exit before letters are issued
