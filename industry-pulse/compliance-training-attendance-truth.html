Compliance Training Attendance Must Be True

If someone else signs in for mandatory compliance learning, the organisation is lying to itself. A certificate without the person creates a dangerous story: leadership thinks the risk is covered when it is not.

Industry Pulse · Compliance & learning integrity

Especially for safety, harassment, anti-bribery, and other mandatory topics, presence is the control — not the PDF. Proxy attendance, shared logins, and “just sign for the team” habits turn training into theatre while risk stays live on the floor.

False completion is a control failure

  • Regulators and boards see completion rates; they do not see who actually sat in the room
  • Incident investigations expose empty certificates quickly
  • Managers who pressure for 100% numbers often incentivise proxies
  • Staff learn that policy is negotiable when attendance is treated as paperwork

Hardening completion integrity

  • Identity checks proportionate to risk — photo ID, unique login, or in-person verification where topics are high stakes
  • No blanket proxy signatures — make substitution an explicit exception, not a norm
  • Manager accountability — team completion truth is a leadership duty, not an HR spreadsheet problem
  • Session design that reduces gaming — reasonable length, clear purpose, scheduled during work time
  • Refreshers before certificates become empty artefacts — outdated completion is not current control

Where HRD Corp claims intersect

Claim and grant processes also depend on real attendance evidence. False lists damage more than culture: they can undermine reimbursement and create a second layer of exposure when documents do not match reality. Treat attendance integrity as both a compliance control and a claim discipline.

Culture signal

How leadership reacts when a false completion is found teaches the organisation whether compliance is real or decorative. Quiet fixes encourage the next proxy. Clear consequence — and support to attend properly — resets the standard.

Practical checklist for HR and L&D

  1. State in policy that proxy attendance for mandatory topics is not allowed
  2. Align managers on completion truth before the campaign starts
  3. Use attendance methods that match risk level (classroom list vs system log)
  4. Spot-check anomalies (same handwriting, same device, impossible schedules)
  5. When false completion is found, correct the record and the behaviour — not only the file

Bottom line

Compliance training attendance must be true. Numbers without people are not assurance — they are a story the organisation tells itself until an incident, audit, or claim review breaks it.
