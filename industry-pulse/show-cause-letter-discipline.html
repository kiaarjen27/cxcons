Show-Cause Letters: Discipline Without Overreach

A show-cause letter is not a verdict. Over-charged language and vague allegations create avoidable conflict.

Facts, rule, response window

State the alleged conduct, the rule or standard involved, and a fair chance to reply — without theatrical accusations or conclusions written as proven guilt.

Drafting mistakes to avoid

  • Conclusions written as proven facts before the reply is heard
  • Kitchen-sink allegations without evidence for each item
  • Unclear response deadline or delivery method
  • Copy-paste templates with wrong names, dates, or policies

Clean structure

  • Particulars of alleged conduct (what, when, where)
  • Policy / rule reference
  • Invitation to explain within a stated time
  • Next process step (without prejudging outcome)
