HRDC Rules and Regulations on Training

Training claims succeed when process discipline is strong

HRD Corp (HRDC) funding helps Malaysian employers upskill their workforce, but claims are approved only when training meets published rules on eligibility, delivery, attendance, documentation, and allowable costs. This article summarises the practical regulations HR and L&D teams should manage before, during, and after a programme.

1. What HRDC claimable training means

HRD Corp claimable training refers to approved learning activities where registered employers may seek reimbursement (or financial support under relevant schemes) for eligible training costs, subject to HRD Corp terms.

In practice, claimability depends on more than the course title. Employer registration, trainee eligibility, programme structure, proof of delivery, and complete supporting documents all affect the outcome.

2. Core compliance pillars employers must manage

Employer eligibility

Confirm the organisation is properly registered and active under HRD Corp requirements before planning claimable programmes.

Trainee eligibility

Ensure participants qualify under the relevant scheme and are correctly recorded for the claim submission.

Approved training scope

Align course content, duration, and delivery mode with current HRD Corp guidelines and scheme conditions.

Evidence of delivery

Keep attendance, materials, invoices, and evaluation records complete, consistent, and ready for audit.

3. Key rules that commonly affect claims

Attendance requirements

Attendance is one of the most practical compliance checkpoints. Incomplete attendance can reduce claimable amounts or affect approval. Employers should track daily attendance accurately and brief participants on minimum attendance expectations before training starts.

Allowable cost principles

Not every related expense is automatically claimable. HRD Corp applies allowable-cost principles that may cover course fees and selected associated costs, depending on scheme rules and supporting documents. Always separate:

  • Clearly claimable training-related costs
  • Conditional costs that need stronger justification/documentation
  • Non-claimable commercial or administrative expenses

Documentation discipline

Strong claims are document-driven. Invoices, attendance records, trainee details, course outlines, and proof of payment/delivery should match each other. Inconsistencies between names, dates, headcount, and amounts are a frequent cause of delay.

4. Employer responsibilities before training

  1. Confirm internal learning objective and target audience.
  2. Verify scheme suitability (public, in-house, or other applicable pathway).
  3. Select a competent provider and clarify claim-support documents needed.
  4. Check dates, venue/mode, trainer profile, and programme structure.
  5. Brief participants on attendance, punctuality, and evaluation requirements.
  6. Prepare internal approval and budget coding before the session runs.

5. Responsibilities during and after training

During

  • Record attendance correctly
  • Ensure training runs as scheduled
  • Capture issues early (absentees, changes)
  • Keep coordinator contact points active

After

  • Compile complete claim file
  • Cross-check names, dates, and amounts
  • Submit within required timelines
  • Archive records for audit readiness

6. Common reasons claims get delayed or reduced

  • Incomplete or inconsistent attendance records
  • Mismatched trainee lists and invoice details
  • Missing supporting documents
  • Costs submitted outside allowable categories
  • Late submission or weak internal coordination
  • Programme details not aligned with scheme conditions

7. Practical checklist for HR and L&D teams

  1. Maintain an internal HRDC claim SOP for every programme type.
  2. Use a pre-training compliance checklist with provider and participants.
  3. Standardise attendance templates and document naming conventions.
  4. Reconcile finance and HR records before submission.
  5. Track claim status and keep a shared archive by programme date.
  6. Train new HR coordinators on documentation and scheme basics.

Frequently asked questions

Is every training course automatically HRDC claimable?

No. Claimability depends on scheme conditions, employer/trainee eligibility, programme structure, and complete supporting documents.

Can in-house training be claimable?

Yes, in-house programmes may be structured under applicable HRDC pathways when requirements are met. Process and documentation still matter.

What should employers prepare first?

Confirm eligibility, choose the right programme format, align internal approvals, and set a clean documentation process before training day.
