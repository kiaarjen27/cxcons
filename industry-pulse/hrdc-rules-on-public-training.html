HRDC Rules on Public Training: What Employers Miss

Sending one or two staff to an external public class feels simpler than organising an in-house workshop — until a late grant submission or unapproved schedule change renders the seat unclaimable. Public training follows distinct HRD Corp lead-time, fee-ceiling, and claim-sequencing rules that employers must lock in before seats are confirmed.

Industry Pulse · HRD Corp compliance & public training

Under the HRD Corp Claimable Courses (HCC / SBL-Khas) framework, public programmes — classes open to participants from multiple employers — operate on per-participant grant logic rather than group rates. Because external providers set fixed public schedules, employers cannot treat grant applications as a last-minute formality after a staff member has already registered.

Core rules that govern public training grants

  • Pre-approval waiting window — grants must be approved before training begins; public classes require at least a 3-calendar-day buffer after approval before commencement (with training starting within 90 days of that window)
  • Zero post-approval amendments — once a grant is approved, dates, venue, trainer, and course details cannot be modified; any change requires cancelling the grant and submitting a fresh application within allowable lead times
  • Confirmed dates and registered providers only — tentative dates and self-sponsored bookings outside HRD Corp registered training providers are not claimable
  • Per-pax Allowable Cost Matrix ceilings — local face-to-face public fees are capped per participant per day (up to RM3,000/pax/day for Focus Area courses and RM1,300/pax/day for Employer-Specific courses, with lower caps for Remote Online Training)
  • Two-stage claim sequence — the training provider submits the course-fee claim first (with T3/ROT attendance and JD14 declaration), and the employer submits any approved trainee allowances or travel claims only after the provider’s claim is approved — all within six months of completion

Where employers run into trouble

Most rejected public training claims fail before class even starts: a manager books a seat two days before the course, HR submits the grant late, or a nominated employee drops out without an approved replacement. Equally common is overlooking the 5-calendar-day response window when HRD Corp raises a single application query — letting the clock expire automatically voids the grant.

Practical operating habits

  • Enforce an internal cut-off requiring all public class nominations at least 10 to 14 days before the training date
  • Collect the provider's quotation, course outline/schedule, and trainer profile before opening e-TRiS
  • Verify whether the programme falls under Focus Area or Employer-Specific ceilings and whether delivery is physical or Remote Online Training (ROT)
  • Monitor e-TRiS daily after submission so any query is answered well inside the 5-calendar-day window
  • Return certified JD14 and attendance logs to the provider promptly after class so the 6-month claim window never tightens

What to do next

Set one internal booking cut-off, one pre-submission document check, and one post-class sign-off owner for all public registrations. Treating public seats with the same grant discipline as in-house programmes eliminates avoidable levy leakage.

Bottom line

Public training offers speed and flexibility for targeted upskilling, but HRD Corp grant rules leave little margin for late paperwork or unapproved changes. Locking dates, documents, and grant approval ahead of the waiting window keeps every public seat clean and claimable.
