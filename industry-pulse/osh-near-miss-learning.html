Near-Miss Learning Beats Luck Narratives

Calling a near-miss “lucky” teaches silence. Calling it data teaches prevention.

Psychological safety is a safety control

People report when the response is fair and useful. Humiliation, jokes, or “don’t make a fuss” kill the next report — and the organisation loses its cheapest warning system.

After a near-miss

  • Thank the reporter
  • Capture facts quickly (what, where, conditions)
  • Fix the condition with an owner and date
  • Share learning without naming-and-shaming

Manager line that helps

“I’m glad you raised this. Let’s make sure it cannot happen the same way again.” Then follow through — empty gratitude trains cynicism.
