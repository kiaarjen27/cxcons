Gifts and Hospitality: Anti-Bribery Practice

Most bribery exposure in ordinary companies is not cinematic corruption — it is festive hampers, vendor meals, and “relationship building” with no register. If hospitality is allowed within limits, recording must be easy and expected. Policies fail when the only option is a heavy form nobody completes, or no register at all.

Anti-bribery and corruption (ABC) frameworks only work when commercial teams can apply them on a normal Tuesday. Staff need thresholds, a simple approval path, and examples of grey zones — not only a policy PDF.

Registers beat memory

If a gift or meal is permitted, the organisation should be able to show what was received, by whom, from whom, and when. Memory is not a control. Informal “everyone knows it’s fine” is how patterns form without oversight.

Keep the register light enough that people use it. A one-line entry with value band, date, and approver is better than a perfect form that stays empty.

Practical controls that staff can follow

  • Clear value thresholds — and what counts toward the threshold (single item vs cumulative from one vendor)
  • Approval route above threshold — named role, not “ask around”
  • Gift / hospitality register discipline — expected, checked, and referenced in audits
  • Vendor communication — share your policy during supplier onboarding so “we didn’t know” is harder
  • No home-delivery workaround — personal address does not remove the obligation

Manager judgement moments

These are the situations where teams need rehearsal, not slogans:

  • Tender window hamper — festive gift to a decision-maker while a vendor evaluation is open
  • Pattern of meals — repeated vendor hospitality that looks like access, not a one-off courtesy
  • “Personal gift” at home — delivered off-site to avoid the office register
  • Post-award celebration paid by the winning vendor — timing matters as much as value

Better default responses: decline or declare; record; escalate when the commercial relationship is active; remove yourself from the decision if independence is compromised.

What “decorative ABC” looks like

  • Policy exists, register does not
  • Thresholds are vague (“reasonable” with no number)
  • Senior staff are informally exempt
  • Training is a once-only e-sign with no scenarios

Staff copy what leaders do. If senior hospitality is invisible, the policy is theatre.

Bottom line

Everyday ABC is operational hygiene: thresholds, registers, and judgement under commercial pressure. Companies that only publish a code of conduct still accumulate risk one hamper at a time.
