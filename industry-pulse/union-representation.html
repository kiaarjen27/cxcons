Streamlining Union Representation: Sole Bargaining Rights

The Industrial Relations Act 1967 was recently updated to address the complexities of having multiple trade unions within a single workplace. The introduction of "Sole Bargaining Rights" aims to simplify the collective bargaining process.

Under the new Section 12A, if multiple unions are recognized by the same employer, the unions may decide among themselves who will hold the sole bargaining rights. If they cannot agree, any party may apply to the Director General for a determination via a secret ballot.

Section 12B further stabilizes this arrangement by stating that once a union obtains sole bargaining rights, no other union can challenge that position for a period of three years. This ensures that collective bargaining—the process of negotiating terms like wages and skill training—can proceed without constant jurisdictional disputes, fostering long-term industrial peace.
