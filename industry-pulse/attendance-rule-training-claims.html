Attendance Rules Can Decide a Claim Outcome

Incomplete attendance evidence can weaken an otherwise strong claim file. For HRD Corp-oriented training, hours completed and how they are recorded often matter as much as the invoice — especially where the 75% rule and proration apply.

Many claim problems are not about the course content. They appear when attendance is partial, inconsistently signed, titled differently from the grant, or collected after the event from memory.

Why attendance sits at the centre of the file

Attendance is the operational proof that training happened for named people over stated hours. Where financial assistance is tied to completion thresholds, weak attendance does not only look untidy — it can reduce recoverable amounts or trigger queries.

  • Common expectation: attendance reaches at least 75% of total training hours
  • Allowance and related amounts may be prorated by attendance completion
  • Half-day and full-day fee logic still depends on real training hours delivered
  • Online or provider-assisted claims may require system attendance declared/signed by both provider and employer where rules require it

What “good attendance evidence” looks like

  • Same programme title as the grant / outline / invoice family
  • Clear training dates and session hours
  • Participant names aligned with the approved headcount story
  • Completion that can be explained if someone left early or missed a block
  • Forms completed close to the event — not reconstructed weeks later

Where T3 or equivalent attendance documentation is expected, treat it as part of delivery, not as admin to finish “when free.”

Ownership: who protects the file

Claims fail operationally when everyone assumes someone else collected the sheet. Assign ownership before the room opens:

  • Before training: HR/L&D confirms the attendance format and name list
  • During training: facilitator or floor owner marks attendance by session block
  • Same day: scan or file the signed record into the programme folder
  • Before claim: finance/HR checks hours vs outline vs invoice narrative

Habits that prevent firefighting

  • Do not start a paid session without the attendance sheet ready
  • Record partial attendance honestly — do not “tidy” the file after the fact
  • For multi-day programmes, capture each day separately
  • Keep one controlled folder per run: outline, attendance, invoice, approvals
  • Review claim outcomes every 30–90 days and fix the recurring break point

Bottom line

Attendance rules can decide a claim outcome because they convert a training event into auditable delivery. Strong content with weak attendance is still a weak file. Treat the sheet as part of the programme design, not as paperwork after the applause.
